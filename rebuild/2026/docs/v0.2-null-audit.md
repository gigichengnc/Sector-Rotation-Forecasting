# v0.2 post-publication methodological audit

## Status

This document supersedes the earlier interpretation of the 2026 coordinate-forecast holdout. The recorded holdout numbers remain historical experiment outputs, but they are no longer treated as evidence that the model discovered forecastable market dynamics.

## What the first reconstruction got right

The 2026 reconstruction did materially improve the original 2025 research process:

- features and targets were timestamped chronologically;
- the train/validation geometry was time ordered and purged;
- `StandardScaler` was fitted inside each training fold/model fit;
- the final holdout protocol prevented holdout targets from entering later model fits;
- predictive and economic-value experiments were separated.

One earlier phrase was too broad, however. The model scaler is fold-local, but the RRG-style rolling coordinate normalization is calculated causally on the chronological price history before the model split. It uses no future timestamp at each point, so this is not look-ahead leakage, but it should not be described as "all preprocessing is fold-local."

## Material post-publication finding

An external review identified a target-construction problem that the original audit missed. The future RRG-style coordinate target contains mechanically predictable structure because its rolling windows overlap information already known at the decision timestamp.

For the default 10-week momentum period:

```text
rs_momentum_raw(t+h)
    = rs_ratio_raw(t+h) / rs_ratio_raw(t+h-10) - 1
```

For every originally reported horizon `h` in `{1, 2, 4, 8}`, the denominator is dated before the decision timestamp `t`. The feature matrix contains lagged RRG-style coordinates from the same historical path. A linear model can therefore recover part of the future target even when the underlying returns contain no serial predictability.

RS-Ratio has a related overlap at short horizons: `rs_ratio_raw(t+h)` is a 10-week relative-return window, so at `h=1` most of the window has already occurred.

This is not conventional leakage. The inputs remain causal. It is a structural-null problem caused by the target definition.

## Synthetic structural-null benchmark

v0.2 adds a deterministic synthetic benchmark with:

- one benchmark geometric random walk;
- 11 synthetic sector geometric random walks;
- contemporaneous beta of 0.9 to the synthetic benchmark;
- independent innovations through time;
- 424 weekly observations;
- the same 52-week holdout geometry;
- 20 weeks of lagged RRG-style coordinate features;
- the same 1/2/4/8-week coordinate targets.

The data-generating process contains no serial predictability by construction.

Across 12 deterministic trials, the legacy coordinate pipeline produced:

| Horizon | Null Linear accuracy | Null persistence | Null edge | Null distance reduction |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 week | 81.0% | 72.8% | +8.2 pp | 31.8% |
| 2 weeks | 73.8% | 63.2% | +10.6 pp | 33.8% |
| 4 weeks | 63.2% | 50.5% | +12.8 pp | 34.6% |
| 8 weeks | 47.4% | 31.9% | +15.5 pp | 35.4% |

The one-week real-data result had been 80.6% versus 74.0%. Because a no-signal process reproduces the same order of magnitude, that result cannot be interpreted as evidence of market predictability.

The null benchmark is implemented in `src/rrg_rebuild/null_benchmark.py` and can be rerun with `scripts/run_synthetic_null.py`.

## Retired interpretation

The statement below remains factually true as a description of the old scoring exercise:

> A fixed linear model outperformed current-state persistence on the recorded 52-week coordinate holdout.

The following inference is retired:

> Therefore recent sector rotation was shown to contain forecastable market information.

Persistence was an inadequate sole benchmark for a target with substantial mechanically predictable overlap.

## Dependence and statistical power

The old reports described 572 rows per horizon as `52 target weeks × 11 sectors`. Those are scored rows, not 572 independent observations.

Dependence enters through at least three channels:

1. all sectors in a week share the same benchmark;
2. sector returns are cross-sectionally correlated;
3. multi-week targets overlap through time, increasingly so at longer horizons.

No formal effective sample size is asserted here. The correct response is to use dependence-aware inference rather than substituting a heuristic independent-n calculation.

Any future headline result must therefore include week-level clustering or block/bootstrap uncertainty with block length at least as large as the forecast horizon.

## Development versus final holdout

The old final report noted that 4-week final accuracy exceeded development accuracy by 6.1 percentage points and called the overall pattern consistent with retained development behaviour.

v0.2 withdraws that interpretation. A holdout being better than development is not evidence of generalisation or stability. The differences can arise from sampling variation, period difficulty, dependence, or regime composition. The table is retained as historical output only.

## Strategy result

The first pre-declared forecast-top-3 strategy remains **not supported by that backtest** because it underperformed all named comparators in the scored path.

That finding must not be strengthened into "there is no alpha." The strategy experiment contains one historical path and no formal uncertainty or power analysis. It neither establishes alpha nor rules out an economically small effect that the experiment lacked power to detect.

## Redesigned primary research target

The next research question uses RRG-style rotation history as features and predicts a quantity that is entirely in the future:

```text
log(asset[t+h] / asset[t])
    - log(benchmark[t+h] / benchmark[t])
```

Every return in this target occurs after the decision timestamp. The initial binary target is whether the sector outperforms the benchmark over the next `h` weeks.

A regularized logistic model is included as a transparent baseline. More complex models are not justified unless they outperform structural/base-rate baselines under the same time-aware protocol and dependence-aware uncertainty analysis.

## Additional audit findings carried into v0.2

- `_causal_rolling_normalize(..., min_periods=1)` emits 100 when the rolling standard deviation is zero. Since quadrant classification uses `>= 100`, a degenerate 100/100 row becomes `Leading`. This behaviour is retained for historical compatibility but should be revisited in a future coordinate-definition revision.
- The legacy linear model uses 40 lagged coordinate features with unregularized OLS. v0.2 does not present that simplicity as a virtue; Ridge or another regularized linear baseline is a natural comparison for any future coordinate-model work.
- Adjusted-close series obtained today can incorporate later corporate-action restatements. This limits point-in-time interpretation of historical Yahoo-based backtests.

## Publication rule from v0.2 onward

1. The old 80.6% figure may appear only as a historical result adjacent to the structural-null correction.
2. Do not describe the old coordinate result as demonstrated market predictability.
3. Do not describe 572 scored rows as 572 independent observations.
4. Do not describe the failed strategy as proof that no alpha exists.
5. Do not call the entire preprocessing pipeline fold-local; distinguish fold-local model scaling from causal pre-split coordinate construction.
6. Any new market-signal claim must exceed a relevant structural null and include dependence-aware uncertainty.
7. Any new final headline claim requires a genuinely prospective evaluation window. The 2025-08-22 to 2026-08-14 holdout is retired for confirmatory use.
